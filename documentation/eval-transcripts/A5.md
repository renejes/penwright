## 1 Introduction

This document is a sample project shipped with vswrite. It is meant to be opened, scrolled through, edited, broken, and rebuilt. Treat it as a playground that happens to also be a small thesis on its own subject: how to write academic texts in an era when AI assistance is part of the workflow whether we acknowledge it or not.

**Who this is for**

There are three groups of readers we had in mind:

- **Researchers and graduate students** who want to draft, structure, and cite their work without context-switching between five tools.
- **Authors of longer non-fiction** who appreciate that scientific tooling (citations, cross-references, footnotes) is also useful outside of academia.
- **Anyone curious** about what it looks like when a writing app is built with AI-assisted workflows in mind from the start, rather than bolted on after the fact.

**What you will see**

Every feature of vswrite — comments, cross-references, footnotes, citations, math, code blocks, figures — appears at least once in this document. The intent is that by the time you reach the conclusion, you have seen the tool's full vocabulary in context. We try not to repeat the user manual; for setup details, configuration, and edge cases see the handbook at vswrite.netlify.app/en/docs.

The substantive thread running through the chapters is the one announced above: how to integrate AI into academic writing without losing the qualities that make academic writing worth reading. We draw on five recent open-access sources to ground the discussion in something more durable than vendor blog posts. All five PDFs ship with the sample under `sources/` so you can hover any citation in the editor and open the original paper inline.

**A note on tone**

vswrite was built around one specific belief: AI is at its best when it takes the tedious parts off the writer's plate — formatting, citation hunting, table conversion, draft polishing — so the writer can spend more time on the part nobody else can do for them, which is having something worth saying. That belief shows up in the rest of this document. You will not find AI maximalism or AI dismissiveness here; both are easy and both are wrong.
